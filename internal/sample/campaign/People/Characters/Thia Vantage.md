# Thia Vantage

*Half-elf, 17. Greywake born. Sixteen when the crown broke.*

## At a glance

Thia was eleven years old on the night of the breaking and she was *in the
Bellhouse*. She has told this table that she was in Greywake, all four of them,
with a straight face and no detail, for nine years. She is not a liar by nature.
She is a liar by accident, in the specific way that you get when you were eleven
and the only adult in the room was screaming at you to go home and you went.

She is a good rogue in the mechanical sense — she is fast, she has a thieves'
cant habit she does not realise she has, and she can pick the lock on a manhole
cover in eleven seconds. She is a bad thief in the narrative sense, because the
one thing she has ever wanted is for the Bellhouse not to be a subject, and she
is the only person at this table who has been inside it.

## What she wants

Hob's money. That is the surface answer and it is not false.

Underneath: she wants to be *asked*. She has spent nine years being the girl who
is not asked. She would rather be an accomplice than a mystery, and she does not
yet know that these are the same thing.

## How to play her

- She lies by **omission**, not by invention. If she does not know, she says
  "I was in Greywake" and means *I was not in the Bellhouse*, which is a
  statement she can defend. Push on the specifics and she folds immediately.
- Thia will take a job she does not want, out loud, as a *joke*, and then do it.
- She is loyal in a way that has cost her everything and that she thinks of as
  merely being unlucky.
- She is fifteen when she lies about her age and gets away with it, and the
  table should be allowed to notice.

## Notes for the game master

She is the party's eyes and hands and she is the campaign's clock — every scene
in the Bellhouse is a scene she has promised herself she is forgetting. Play her
narrator as evasive under pressure. When she finally says the true thing, it
should be in a place where saying it out loud costs her a friend, not in a
confession scene.

```secret id=5d2e8ba604c7 visibility=private author=thia created=2026-07-19T22:15:00Z title="What Thia saw in the Bellhouse"
Thia was behind the lectern when it broke, because she had been sent to fetch
the archive ladder and had gone up it to look at the crown because nobody ever
told her not to.

She was not hurt. That is the part nobody believes.

What she saw is this: the Compact was not made of nine clauses. It was made of
**ten**, and the tenth was written on the inside of the crown where only a person
standing behind the lectern could read it, in letters no larger than a fingernail
and in a hand she recognised as her mother's.

The tenth clause names a person, not a city. It is a release of obligation, and
it is unconditional, and it was there before the Compact was sworn.

Thia has read it approximately four hundred times since. She can recite it. She
will not say it out loud to this table until the fifth session, and when she does
she will be interrupted, and the interruption is the last real choice the campaign
gives anybody.
```
