# Hollowfen

*The high ground at the end of the [[Salt Road]]. Four hundred people, a chapel,
a single inn, and a chapterhouse in the water.*

## The shape of it

The hills are the oldest ground in the fen and the only part of it anybody would
call a hill. The road comes up out of the mud at a stone gate that has a date on
it and a Compact mark, and everything above that gate is dry in any year.

Above the gate: four hundred people, a chapel that is also a tide-warden's house,
one inn that is also the ferry booking office, and the **long house** where
[[Ibb Oth-Kerrin]] lives and refuses visitors on the fourth day of the week.

## The Bellhouse

A mile east of the gate, at the head of a tidal creek, is a **chapterhouse of the
Compact**: two storeys, a copper-capped roof, and a precinct that flooded in the
first week of the breaking and has been under six feet of water ever since.

The water goes down in a dry autumn. It is not dry this autumn. The party are
going to have about nine days of a falling tide, and the crossing is possible for
perhaps four hours a day at the end of it, and only on the causeway-step.

Everything the campaign is about is in that building and nothing has moved in
nine years, because nothing can get in and nothing has gone out.

The building is not flooded. That is the game's central fact and it is on
nobody's page.

The chapterhouse was built on a **culvert**. The Compact's engineers ran a
sewer under the precinct in the year of the signing, and they built it well, and
the building was designed around it: the copper was laid precisely so the
record would stay dry. At the breaking, the crown went into the water and the
water went into the culvert and the culvert went into the building, and the
building **drained**.

At low water the third gallery is walkable, chest-deep, and dry above the knee.
Nine years of tide have not touched it.

Things the party will find up there, in this order:

1. **The copper case**, on the reading table, sealed, with the Compact's text
   in it in nine hands. Intact. This is the objective and it is not guarded.
2. **The iron doors**, standing open, which should be shut. They were opened
   from the inside at 23:11 on the breaking night and nothing closed them, and
   that is a fact the table will find before it finds anything else.
3. **Eleven sets of boot-prints** in nine years of silt, all the same, going in
   and not coming out. They are [[The Ferryman]]'s. He goes in at low water,
   every time, and reads, and leaves. The party can work out how long he has
   been doing that, and it is the single most disturbing thing available.
4. **The ledger's ninth volume**, which is not in the Bellhouse and is in
   [[Greywake]] under [[Kess Varro]]'s desk, and the fact that it is missing
   from here is what tells the party somebody took it and did not steal it.

**The thing under the reading table** is for the game master and nobody else:
there is a body, it is nine years old, it is wearing a fen-watch coat, and its
hand is closed around a strip of bell-bronze. Do not resolve it in session
three. Resolve it in the same session the party decides what the fen is going
to be, and not before, because a corpse in a copper room is a puzzle and a
corpse in a room where four people are choosing a future is a *reason*.

The party can find the body's identity from the fourth book in
[[The Tide-Clerk Ledger]] in one lookup, and the eleventh set of prints is
[[The Ferryman]]'s, and those two facts together are the whole of the third
bell without anybody having to lie about anything.

## Who is here

- [[The Ferryman]] — at the tideline, every day the tide allows, every year.
- [[Ibb Oth-Kerrin]] — at the long house, on three days a week.
- The Ferrymaster's widow, who runs the inn, who is not important and who the
  game master should absolutely use to deliver one piece of information and then
  never speak again.

## Running it

Hollowfen is the campaign's last quiet place and its first dangerous one. The
party arrives on the road, crosses the fen, and has four sessions' worth of
material in a building they can only enter when the tide is out.

**The tide is the clock.** Do not forget it, and do not let the party forget it
either. The window is four hours, twice a day, and shrinking. The moment the
tide is in, the Bellhouse is nine feet under and the way out is a mile of mud
that is four feet under.
