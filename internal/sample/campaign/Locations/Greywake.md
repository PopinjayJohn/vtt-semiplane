# Greywake

*Eleven thousand people on a river-delta island. One tide-street, one inn, one
watch-house, and a road that opens forty days a year.*

## The shape of it

Greywake is not a town on the Salt Road. It is a town because of the Salt Road,
and it is a town *despite* the other three hundred and twenty days, which is
what eleven thousand people live on.

Three streets and a wharf. The **Kettle** at the top, the **fen-watch house** at
the bottom, and the **Grey House** halfway between, which is a converted toll
booth and is the smallest religious building in the fen and by some distance the
most influential. The tide-locked, and every year between it and the water, four
hundred yards of mud that is a road in September and a drowning in November.

## What is actually happening here

Nobody is fighting. That is the thing about the fen, and it is why the campaign
is difficult to pitch in one line.

The eight cities around Greywake have not spoken to it in nine years. They have
also not cut the causeway, closed the trade, or moved their boats. They have
conducted themselves, with enormous discipline, as though the Compact were still
in force while acting as though it were not. This is the *quiet*, and it holds
because it costs everybody nothing, and it is about to stop costing nothing.

> [!warning] The causeway is the whole campaign
> Nine days from now the autumn tide opens the road. Greywake either runs the
> lottery or the road does not open, and if the road does not open, the fen
> starts to empty, and the fen emptying is a slow thing that will not be
> finished within the campaign and will not stop.

## Running it

Greywake is a hub, not a dungeon. Two or three scenes, never more. The
interesting thing in every Greywake scene is that *everyone* wants the same
narrow thing and will not say what it is.

Do not let the party spend a session in town. Greywake works best as the place
they keep having to come back to, and the causeway as the place they keep having
to leave.
