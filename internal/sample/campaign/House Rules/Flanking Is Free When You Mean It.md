# Flanking Is Free When You Mean It

*Agreed at table. Replaces the published requirement that a flanked creature
must be using its reaction.*

## The rule

> **Two allies adjacent means advantage.** No Dodge, no Disengage, no reaction to
> spend. Position is the whole requirement.

The rule as written is below, in the fence. It is a `table` secret, which
means every signed-in reader at the table can read it — see
[[Writing secrets]].

```secret id=c53b82ebf6d4 visibility=table author=dungeonmaster created=2026-01-17T20:10:00Z title="A flank with no reaction"
Any creature with two allies adjacent to its target has advantage on melee attacks
against that target. The target does not get to Dodge, does not get to
Disengage, and does not get a reaction to spend.

The only requirement is positioning: two allies, adjacent, both alive. Enemies
use the same rule, and a wolf pack flanking the party is not a special tactic,
it is two wolves adjacent.
```

## Why

The published flanking rule is a tax on the fiction. Every time a monster
"Dodges" because a player moved round it, somebody at the table has to stop and
ask whether it *wanted* to, and the answer is almost always no — it wanted to
eat the wizard. Removing the tax makes positioning actually matter, which is the
entire reason positioning is a thing.

It also makes the *wizard's* job better. A caster who cannot flank wants to be
in the back row forever. A caster who can flank is a threat the party has to
plan around, and the back row stops being free.

## What it costs

- **Pack tactics become the default.** A party of four will be a scissor
  movement every single combat, every time, without being told. This is the
  price and it is a real one: the fight gets easier and more predictable.
- **Monsters need geometry, not reactions.** Give the enemy a reason to spend
  its reaction on something else — an overwatch, a defensive spell — or it feels
  like the monster is cheating. Pre-mark one enemy with a reaction already spent
  per combat and nobody will ever complain about it again.

## In practice

Two allies adjacent, advantage, no roll to interrupt it. If only one ally is
adjacent, no advantage. If the target is huge, "adjacent" means adjacent to a
part of the creature that can be attacked, and that is a conversation the table
has once and then never again.

Enemies use the same rule. A wolf pack flanking the party is not a special
tactic, it is two wolves adjacent, and the party will recognise it immediately
because it is what they do.
