# A Natural Twenty Is A Choice

*Agreed at table. Applies to every attack roll that naturally rolls a 20.*

## The rule

> **A natural 20 is a licence to choose what happens**, not a number. Three
> options, and the table picks the expensive one on purpose.

The rule as written is below, in the fence. It is a `table` secret, which
means every signed-in reader at the table can read it — see
[[Writing secrets]].

```secret id=b42a71dae5c3 visibility=table author=dungeonmaster created=2026-01-17T20:05:00Z title="A natural twenty, as the rule"
A natural 20 on an attack roll is not an automatic hit. It is a licence to choose
one of three things:

1. **The hit** — normal damage, no crit multiplier.
2. **The consequence** — the attack lands and something changes on the
   battlefield that damage alone would not have done.
3. **The crit** — double dice, as published, and the expensive option, because
   it is the one that ends encounters.

A natural 1 still misses. There is no symmetrical option, and there is not going
to be one.
```

## Why

Crits end fights. A character with a good crit rate at level 3 is a different
game from the same character without one, and it is a worse game for everyone
who is not that character. Making the 20 a *licence* rather than a *result*
puts the decision where the interesting material is: in the fiction.

It also means a player can have a great turn without their dice deciding it,
which is the whole reason anyone plays.

## In practice

Say the three options out loud the first time it comes up, and then never again
— after two sessions the table will not need it. If a player rolls a 20 and
looks at the game master expectantly, that is a table that has not yet internalised
it, and the correct move is to ask them which one they want.

A natural 1 is unchanged: the attack misses. There is no symmetrical option
here, and there never will be — see [[Nobody Rounds Down]] for what we do
instead.
