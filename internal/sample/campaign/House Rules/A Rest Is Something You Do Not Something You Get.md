# A Rest Is Something You Do, Not Something You Get

*Agreed at table. The published short and long rest timings are unchanged; what
changes is who decides they happened.*

## The rule

> **A rest is a scene a player starts, not a resource that ticks.** A long rest
> needs a place with a name.

The rule as written is below, in the fence. It is a `table` secret, which
means every signed-in reader at the table can read it — see
[[Writing secrets]].

```secret id=d64c93fca7e5 visibility=table author=dungeonmaster created=2026-01-24T19:00:00Z title="A rest, as the rule"
A rest is a scene, and the player initiates it. Nobody takes a short rest because
six seconds have passed; a player says they are stopping, and then the table
plays the stopping.

A long rest must have a location with a name. A named inn, a named camp, a
named ruin somebody has slept in before. "We rest" in the middle of the road is
a short rest, and the game master will say so.

The published timings are unchanged. The clock the campaign runs on is not
stopped by any of this: nine days to the tide is nine days, and resting spends
one.
```

## Why

The published rest is a resource with a clock on it, and clocks create the exact
behaviour this rule is trying to remove: the party rests *when they are low*
and not when they are *in a place*, and the fiction degrades into pacing.

This rule makes rest cost a scene's worth of table time, which is the largest
thing it costs, and it buys back the thing the campaign is actually about: a
party that *chooses* where it sleeps is a party making a statement, and
[[The Salt Road]] is a campaign about a road somebody has to choose to walk.

It also does something the numbers cannot. A long rest at an inn with a name is
a place to put a player, and a player at a table with a name starts talking.

## In practice

- Six seconds is not a rest. It is a *paragraph*. If a player says "I take a
  short rest", the game master says "good — where, and who is on watch?"
- Long rest in a named place: full restore, and the table gets the scene.
- Long rest in a nameless place: short rest. Do not argue about it; the rule is
  the rule and it is a rule the table wrote.
- **The campaign clock does not stop for rests.** Nine days to the tide is nine
  days. Resting costs the party a window, and the table will feel that on the
  [[Salt Road]] long before the game master has to say it.
