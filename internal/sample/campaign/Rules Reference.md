# The Greywake Rules

*The rules of this campaign, in one page, written down before the first session
so that nobody has to remember.*

> [!note] These are the *campaign's* rules
> The ten rules this table plays by are in [[House Rules]], one page each, and
> they are a different thing entirely. This page is the world: the tide, the
> road, the quiet, and the three numbers that drive the campaign. Read it out
> loud in the first ten minutes. It takes nine minutes and it saves an hour.

## 1. The tide

The delta rises about a foot and a half on the autumn tide and holds for forty
days. This is the whole reason [[Greywake]] is a town and not a place people
used to live.

Three states matter and the table should name them out loud when they change:

- **Low water.** The mud is mud. Cartable on the [[Salt Road]]; crossable to
  the Bellhouse by the causeway-step for about **four hours**, twice a day.
- **Making.** The mud is soft and the step is gone. Nothing crosses.
- **High water.** Eleven feet at the precinct, four feet on the fen. The
  Bellhouse is unreachable and anything out there is in serious trouble.

**The tide is the campaign's clock.** Not a countdown — a *window*. There is no
session where the party cannot leave, and there is no session where the party
cannot get back, and every session that runs long is a session that costs them
the next one.

## 2. The road

The [[Salt Road]] is eleven miles, flat, and takes **two days by cart, three on
foot**. The party is on foot. On foot, a road this flat is a place where nothing
can be seen until it is close, and that is deliberate.

The road has **twenty-two waymarkers**, a bronze bell on each, maintained by a
levy nobody has stopped paying. The **eighth from Greywake has no bell on it.**

Nobody in Greywake talks about which one. When the party mentions it, play
[[Kess Varro]] going quiet, and do not explain.

## 3. The lottery

The third clause of the Greywake Compact is the **tide-lottery**: the use of the
causeway is allotted among the nine signatories by lot, sound in every quarter.

The lot is sound. That is not decoration — it is the hinge of the whole campaign,
and it is the clause nobody in Greywake can stop saying. It is
[[Kess Varro]]'s office. It is the reason the fen has boats.

If the Compact is discharged, the lottery falls to no one, and the road does not
open, and the fen starts to empty. Nobody has told the fen this in so many words
and everybody there knows it. See [[The Ninefold Oath]].

## 4. The quiet

The fen has been quiet for nine years. **Quiet** is the operative word: not
hostile, not embargoed, not silent by accident. Eight cities behaving as though
a contract were in force while acting as though it were not.

It is enforced by [[Dame Alis Rennard]] and the fen-watch, on the Assembly's
pushing, and it costs forty-one arrests in nine years and no injuries at all.
The Assembly pushed for it because an argument would have been worse. This is
the most defensible thing anybody in the campaign has done and it produced this
situation.

The quiet has a **review date**: the start of the tide. If the party is still on
the causeway when the lotteries are drawn, the arrangement becomes something
under investigation rather than a continuity, and the fen notices.

## 5. The three numbers

Keep these visible. They are the campaign's arithmetic and they are meant to
hurt.

| Number | What it is | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Eleven thousand** | people on the fen island | The population of every other city, combined, if the road stays shut |
| **Two** | people held under the Compact's guarantee of passage | Nineteen and twenty-one years old, held on the sixth city's account, and the party does not find out until session four |
| **Nine** | days until the autumn tide | Four hours a day at the end of it, to reach the document |

## 6. Reading the city

Five questions a player in Greywake can always ask and the answer to each:

1. *Does the road open?* — **Nobody knows.** Not hedging: the fen has been
   running the lottery for nine years on a contract nobody can prove is dead.
2. *Is the Compact in force?* — **Nobody knows**, and the two people who might
   know are [[Rev. Adalet Ferrum]] and [[Ibb Oth-Kerrin]], and they will give
   you opposite answers, both carefully.
3. *What happens to the fen?* — **Nobody will say it first.** They will say
   everything around it. The fifth question gets it: *"and if the road does not
   open, what happens to the eleven thousand?"* Somebody will answer that.
4. *Who is the Assembly?* — **A house on the middle street with a lending
   library**, and the most helpful people in town.
5. *Who is Hob?* — **A publican with eleven boats**, and the only person in
   Greywake who will tell the party the truth about anything, because he has no
   reason not to.
