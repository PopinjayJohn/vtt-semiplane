# Halting Rules (not published)

> [!info] Why this page is quiet
> The rule on this page is `private` and authored by the game master, so it is
> readable by the game master, by an administrator, and by the owners of this
> page. Nobody else. That is not a judgement about the table — it is a rule the
> group agreed to keep in their own hands, and it is the playtest question
> [[No One Here Dies Of Arithmetic]] opened. See [[Writing secrets]] for what the
> visibility means and why it is a fence rather than a frontmatter key.

## The rule

> **A party with a downed member is in two scenes at once, and both of them
> are yours.** The combat and the conversation after it, running at the same
> time, for as long as somebody is on the ground.
>
> What is and is not allowed on the floor is in the fence.

The rule is in the fence. This one is a `private` secret, which means it is
**not** readable by the whole table — see [[Writing secrets]].

## Why

This is the playtest question that [[No One Here Dies Of Arithmetic]] created
and that we did not answer when we agreed it. A rule that stops killing
characters moves the difficulty from the fight to the *aftermath*, and the
aftermath is where a table is most likely to lose somebody.

So: a downed character is not a ragdoll to be carried. They are a person in the
room, and the room is still dangerous, and the game master has to do two
narratives at once. That is expensive. It is also the best two scenes in the
campaign, and it only happens if somebody at the table insists on it.

## In practice

Everything else about this rule is in the fence as well, including the number
that matters and what a downed character is still allowed to do. It is not on
this page because the page is not the right place for it: this is a rule about
the game master's job, and a job the table can read is a job the table starts
supervising.

```secret id=07a29f54e8d1 visibility=private author=dungeonmaster created=2026-01-25T18:00:00Z title="Halting rules, for the game master"
When a player character is down, the scene does not move on without them.

A party with a downed member is in a fight *and* in a negotiation, and the game
master runs both. The combat continues. So does the talking, because the downed
character hears everything said within ten feet, and anything said further away
is the game master's problem to justify later.

Ten feet is the honest number. Beyond it, a downed character hears the *shape*
of a conversation and not its content.

A downed character can still act. Call out. Grab a leg. Spend a spell slot on
something with a verbal component, which is how a caster who goes down loudly
tells everyone in thirty feet exactly where they are.
```
