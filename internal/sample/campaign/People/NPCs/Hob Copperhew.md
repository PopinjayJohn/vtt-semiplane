# Hob Copperhew

*Halfling, 58. Keeps the Copper Kettle. Owns eleven boats.*

## Who he is

Hob has run the Copper Kettle for twenty-two years and has spent all of them
being the second-most-powerful person in [[Greywake]] in a way that
[[Dame Alis Rennard]] does not have to notice. He is not a villain and he is not
what he appears to be, which is a jolly publican with a good ear and no
ambition.

He is an owner. The Kettle is the front; the eleven boats are the business, and
the boats are the reason a fen town is a town rather than a place people used to
live. Every autumn, forty days, the causeway opens, and the boats go out, and
without the causeway the boats are furniture.

## What he wants

Hob hires the party because he genuinely cannot carry the letter himself: the
letter is for the Bellhouse, and going to the Bellhouse means being seen going,
and Hob is seen going everywhere. He would do it himself. He is sixty-eight
next month and he still does most things himself.

What Hob actually wants is for the letter to be delivered and for nobody to
notice. He has not achieved that in nine attempts and the letter is now four
years old.

## How to play him

- Hears everything, repeats almost nothing, and the almost-nothing is chosen.
- Pays in coin, exactly on time, slightly too much — a man who does not want
  anyone asking what the job really was.
- Genuinely kind to the fen-watch recruits and genuinely ruthless about the
  lottery. Both of those are true and neither cancels the other.
- Will offer the party a table and a warm fire and one more drink than is wise,
  in this order, as a way of finding out what they know.

## For the game master

Hob is a good first session character. He hands the party a job, a fee, and a
warning he does not know is a warning: *"It's dry work. Nothing happens on the
Salt Road."* On the Salt Road, something happens. See [[The Salt Road Ambush]].

> [!tip] The four previous couriers
> Four people have carried a letter to the Bellhouse for Hob. Three came back.
> The fourth is [[The Ferryman]], and Hob has never been told that the fourth is
> running the tides.
