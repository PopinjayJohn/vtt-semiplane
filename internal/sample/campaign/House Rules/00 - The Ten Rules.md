# The Ten Rules

*This table's house rules, one page each. Ten of them, argued about at a
kitchen table, and the whole of the folder is listed here because a folder with
no index in it is a folder nobody trusts.*

> [!info] Six are open, three are not published, and one is not agreed
> The six marked **published** can be read by anybody signed in. The three marked
> **not published** are between the game master and the owners of the page — and
> one of those three was written by a *player*, which is the only reason a
> player can read a private house rule at all. The last one is not on the table's
> books yet and says so.
>
> The mechanism is a fence, not a frontmatter key, and it is the same one every
> other secret in this vault uses. [[Writing secrets]] has the syntax; what
> follows is only which fence is on which page.

## The one to read out loud first

[[No One Here Dies Of Arithmetic]] is a real departure from the published rules
and it changes what a session feels like. It was written after a character died
in a corridor and ended a session, and the table consented to it in advance on
the strength of that. It is also the rule most likely to be revised, and the
revision will be because of a player, not the game master.

The world itself — the tide, the road, the lottery, the quiet — is on
[[The Greywake Rules]], and none of that is a house rule. House rules are about
how *we* play; the Greywake rules are about what *happens*.
