# The Quiet Assembly

*A religious order with nine houses, four hundred members, and one question it
has spent thirty years asking: does an obligation survive its own impossibility?*

## What they want

**The dissolution.** Not a reform, not a renegotiation, not a new compact — the
permanent and written end of the Greywake Compact, in a form that no court and
no assembly can revive.

The Assembly's position is theologically simple and legally unprecedented: an
obligation that cannot be performed is discharged by the obligor's own act, and
nine years of silence *is* that act. Nine cities not speaking is not a
suspension. It is a discharge, and it has happened whether anyone signed it or
not.

## How they work

**Hospitality.** This is not a metaphor. The Assembly's method is to put you
somewhere warm, feed you, lend you a book you cannot refuse, and be so
genuinely pleasant that by the time they ask you for something you have already
agreed to twice.

Every Assembly house in the campaign runs a lending library, a soup, and a
grumble-therapy for anyone who walks in without a reason. Rev.
[[Adalet Ferrum]] is the best of them and she is not hiding it.

## What they will not do

They will not lie to you about a fact, because the order's whole authority is
that it does not need to. Adalet will tell you the dissolution closes the road.
She will tell you it closes the road. She will tell you *before* you sign
anything.

This is what makes them dangerous and what makes the campaign work: the party
cannot treat them as villains, because villains lie about costs and this one
tells you the cost and then does the thing anyway, for reasons that are good.

## The uncomfortable part

The Assembly is right that the Compact is a lie told slowly, and the party will
probably agree, and the [[Rules Reference]] is where the disagreement is written
down so that the table can have it out loud.

They are also, in the campaign's own timeline, holding two people who were
hostages under the Compact's guarantee of passage. The party does not learn this
in session three. They learn it in session four, from [[Nettle Ninefingers]],
and the reason it is not in session three is that the Assembly would not have
denied it.

## For the game master

Do not make the Assembly sinister. Make them *effective*, and make them
effective in a way that costs the table something specific and named. The cost
falls on [[Kess Varro]] and on the fen, and the party should be able to name it
by session three even if they do not have to act on it until session four.

> [!tip] The funding
> In session three the Assembly offers to fund [[Sera Nighthollow]]'s treatise.
> This is correct. It is also the single most effective move in the campaign, and
> it works because it is honest: the Assembly genuinely wants the Compact
> dissolved, and a published argument that it is a legal fiction is a weapon the
> Assembly is happy to hand the table and watch them use. Take the money. Find
> out later.
