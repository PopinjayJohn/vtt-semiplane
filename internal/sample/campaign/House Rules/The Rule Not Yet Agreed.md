# The Rule Not Yet Agreed (not yet put to anyone)

> [!info] Why this page is quiet
> This fence is `dm`, which is a different thing from the two unpublished
> rules above it: a `dm` secret is readable by game masters and administrators
> and by **nobody else** — not by the owners of the page, and not by the author
> if the author is a player. The unpublished rules in this folder use both
> visibilities on purpose, so the difference is something a reader can see in a
> vault rather than something they have to take on documentation. See
> [[Writing secrets]].

## The rule

> **There is a structural deadline and nobody has agreed what happens at it.**
> What the game master is considering, and the three open questions that stop
> it being a rule, are in the fence.

The rule is in the fence. This one is a `dm` secret, which means it is **not**
readable by the whole table — see [[Writing secrets]].

## Why

This has not been put to anybody and is not going to be without a conversation
first. It is here because the campaign has a hard structural deadline — the
autumn tide, nine days, a four-hour window — and a hard deadline plus a group
that enjoys talking turns into a group that never decides, and a group that
never decides is a group that has a bad time and blames the game.

The intended use is not as a threat. It is as a **permission to stop**: a
campaign that ends unfinished on purpose is a better outcome than a campaign that
is dragged past the point where anybody is enjoying it, and the way to make that
available is to say it out loud before anybody needs it.

## Why it is a draft

It is a draft because three questions have not been answered, and the answers
are not the game master's to give alone: it is a rule about pacing that spends
the table's time, so the table has to be asked. What the questions are, and
what the game master is considering, is in the fence.

```secret id=3ad5c2871b04 visibility=dm author=dungeonmaster created=2026-08-01T09:00:00Z title="The ninth day"
The ninth day is the last day. If the party has not decided by the time the tide
turns, the campaign ends in the middle of the next session, at the table,
without a resolution, and the table plays out what they were doing when it
stopped.

Open questions, which is why this is a draft:

- Does the *table* agree to this, or only the game master? Only the table can.
  It is a rule about the game master's pacing that spends the table's time, and
  that is backwards.
- What counts as "decided"? Signing a superseding Compact, dissolving the
  original, walking away, or the fifth city swearing on its own without any of
  them.
- If it fires, is that a good ending? It is a real ending and it is the one this
  campaign is most likely to have, and the group should say out loud whether it
  would rather know that in advance or find out.
```
