# No One Here Dies Of Arithmetic

*Agreed at table, 2026-01-17. Read this one out loud before the first session.
It is the only rule in this folder that changes what a game feels like, and the
table is entitled to consent to it in advance rather than discover it in the
middle of a fight.*

## The rule

> **A character at 0 hit points is down and stable, not dying.** Nobody dies of
> arithmetic. They wake at 1, and the table decides what it cost.

The rule as written is below, in the fence. It is a `table` secret, which
means every signed-in reader at the table can read it — see
[[Writing secrets]].

```secret id=a31f60c9d4b2 visibility=table author=dungeonmaster created=2026-01-17T20:00:00Z title="No One Here Dies Of Arithmetic"
A character reduced to 0 hit points is not dying. They are down and stable, and they
get up at the start of the next dawn, or after an hour of rest, whichever comes
first.

There are no death saves here. There is no massive-damage instant death. There
is no *death*. A character who reaches 0 is unconscious, stable, and at 1 hit
point when they come round.

**The table decides what waking up cost them.** Not the game master. The table.
A consequence is offered, the player picks it, and it is permanent and written
on the character page.
```

## Why we play it this way

We were three sessions in and [[Orrin Blackhall]] died to a wolf in a corridor,
and the session ended, and two people went home unhappy, and neither of them had
a story they wanted to tell on the way home. That is the argument. It is not a
mechanical argument, and the fix is not mechanical either, which is why it is
written down.

## What it costs

Everything. Say this part out loud too:

- **The villain is not scarier.** A thing that cannot kill you is a thing you
  can plan around, and the campaign loses its teeth in a way that is genuinely
  hard to feel until a player exploits it. Expect a player to *try* to get
  their character executed within the first month.
- **The stakes move to consequence.** Every scene of real danger now becomes a
  question about what the party gives up to win, not whether somebody survives.
  That is a better game for this table. It is a worse game for a table that
  wanted the sword of Damocles.
- **It is reversible only by the table.** If the table changes its mind, the
  rule changes. It is not written into the system and it cannot be smuggled
  back in as a natural 20 or a healing word nobody earned.

## How it is played

1. A character hits 0. They go down. Say so plainly and move on — do not
   linger, do not make it a scene, the scene comes later.
2. Everything else in the fight continues normally. The downed character is out,
   not dead, and the other three are still in trouble.
3. When they come round, they are at 1. The table then asks the game master one
   question: **what did this cost?** Not a penalty roll. A narrative cost, named
   and specific, and the player chooses it from what the game master offers.
   Usually: someone is left behind, something is spent that cannot be bought
   back, or a debt is incurred that the campaign will collect.
4. The cost is permanent. It goes on the character page and it is still true in
   session six.

## This is not a published rule

The *Player's Handbook* has death saves, three successes and three failures, and
it has a damage threshold that kills outright, and both of those are gone here.
If somebody at the table plays a character who would have died, the correct
response is *"that is exactly the rule we are running"* and then ask the table
what it cost. It is a real departure, it is a deliberate one, and it is the
first thing to revisit if the campaign stops being tense.
