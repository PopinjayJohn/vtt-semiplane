# Kess Varro

*Halfling, 61. Keeps the tide-ledger at Greywake. The youngest thing in the room
and the only one who reads the water instead of the paper.*

## At a glance

Kess is the tide-clerk: the person who decides, forty days a year, which of the
thousands of boats gets the causeway first. It is a small office. It is also the
single most politically important appointment in the fen, and everyone in
[[Greywake]] knows it, and Kess has spent forty years being extremely careful
about that.

Kess worships the Drowned Mother, which is a river religion that does not
guarantee anything and is honest about it. Kess believes the Compact is still
binding, has always believed it, and thinks the whole campaign is a slowly
unfolding argument for a position Kess already holds.

Kess is also the only character who is genuinely good at their job, and the
table will not notice this for two sessions.

## What she wants

Kess wants the autumn lottery to run. Not for ideology — because the causeway
being open for forty days is the only thing that makes a fen town a town, and
Kess has watched the closure of two others.

Kess will fight for the road, will lie for it, and will put herself in the water
for it, and she will not do any of those things in a way that reads as heroism
until the third one.

## How to play her

- Counts. If a number matters, Kess is already counting it and will say so.
- Speaks to small people as equals and to large people as a clerk, and is not
  aware of doing it.
- Never raises her voice; gets quieter instead, and the table should notice when
  she stops being quiet.
- Will take a personal loss to avoid a public one, and the campaign is built
  around someone making her choose.

## Notes for the game master

Kess is the campaign's early-warning system. Give her the numbers before the
table has any idea what they mean and she will put them in the table's hands as
ordinary bookkeeping, and the table will not notice until the numbers are the
whole argument.

She is also the honest cost of the campaign. The Quiet Assembly's plan is not
wrong and it is not cruel; it is *correct*, and it closes the road, and Kess's
life is the measure of what correct costs. The game master does not have to say
that. Just let Kess do the arithmetic out loud, once, in session four.

> [!note] For the sixth session, if the table gets that far
> The lotteries run on Kess's authority and only Kess's. If the party ends up
> holding the third clause, they do not get to run the road — they have to ask
> Kess, and Kess will ask them why they should be allowed to.
