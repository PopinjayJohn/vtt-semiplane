# The Rule About Names (not published)

> [!info] Why this page is quiet
> `private`, authored by the game master. Readable by the game master, by an
> administrator, and by the owners of this page. Of the unpublished rules in
> this folder it is the only one about the game master rather than about the
> game.

## The rule

> **A name, once established, is load-bearing.** What that means in practice
> for a game master holding nine named NPCs and one liar is in the fence, and
> nowhere else on this page.

The rule is in the fence. This one is a `private` secret, which means it is
**not** readable by the whole table — see [[Writing secrets]].

## Why

The alternative is worse and it is worse quietly. A name that changes between
sessions is a bookkeeping error from the game's point of view and a betrayal
from the fiction's, and players forgive a hard fight in a way they do not
forgive being told that the innkeeper they insulted in session two is now
called something else.

The rule, and the four cases that decide most of it, are in the fence.

```secret id=29c4b1760af3 visibility=private author=dungeonmaster created=2026-02-28T16:00:00Z title="Names, once spoken"
An NPC who has been named aloud in front of the party keeps that name for the rest
of the campaign — even if they are lying, even if the name is not theirs, even
if they turn out to be a different person with the same name.

Renaming an established NPC to a new one is a cost, and the cost is paid in
table time: the new name is introduced, the old one is explained, and somebody
will get it wrong on purpose for a while.

Lying names are the good case. Thia Vantage says she was in Greywake for nine
years; the game master writes *Vantage* on her page and does not change it when
the truth arrives.

Two NPCs may share a name. That is a plot point, not an error.
```
