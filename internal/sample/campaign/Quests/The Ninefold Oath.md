# The Ninefold Oath

*The campaign's spine. Deliver a letter, find a document, choose which of the
eight bad futures the fen gets.*

## The situation

The Greywake Compact was made of **nine clauses**, one per river-city, bound in
iron and sworn in a single hour. The crown was the mechanism; the clauses were
what it enforced. On the night of the breaking the crown broke and the clauses
went out into the world as nine pieces of a dead thing.

The Compact's text survives. It is in the Bellhouse at [[Hollowfen]], under six
feet of water, in a copper case sealed against the flood, and it has been
readable the whole time.

The **third clause** is the tide-lottery clause: it governs who gets the autumn
causeway, and it rotates, and it is the one clause that has to be *run* to have
any effect at all. The other eight are dormant. The third one is next.

## What the party is actually doing

Three jobs, in this order, and the party is only ever told about the first one:

1. **Carry Hob's letter to the Bellhouse.** A real errand, a real fee, and a
   real reason for four people who have never worked together to be walking in
   the same direction.
2. **Get the document out.** Which nobody has asked them to do and which
   [[Ibb Oth-Kerrin]] is far too proud to ask for.
3. **Decide.** Not fight for a thing. Decide *which clause the fen spends*. This
   is the campaign, and it happens in session four or five, and by then the
   party will have opinions and no legal standing whatsoever.

## The factions' positions

| | Wants | If they win |
|---|---|---|
| [[The Quiet Assembly]] | Written dissolution of all nine clauses | The Compact cannot be revived. The causeway has no governing clause. The road does not open, permanently. |
| [[Hob & Daughters]] | The road to open this year | The lottery runs. Nobody's position on the Compact has changed and nobody has asked. |
| [[Dame Alis Rennard]] | The quiet to be explained | The quiet is over. Greywake is a town in a nine-city argument. |
| [[Sera Nighthollow]] | A citation that fails in public | The Compact is a legal fiction, on the record, funded by the Assembly. |
| [[Orrin Blackhall]] | To be discharged personally | See his page. It is not on the table. |

> [!danger] There is no good answer
> Every one of these is defensible and two of them hurt people. That is the
> point. A campaign in which one option is obviously right is a campaign in which
> the players are executing a plan rather than making one.

```secret id=a71b3e9d04c6 visibility=table author=dungeonmaster created=2026-07-22T15:30:00Z title="The third clause, as the whole table is entitled to read it"
**Third Clause, of the Greywake Compact.**

*The autumn causeway shall stand open from the first of the tide to the last,
forty days, unclosed. The use of the causeway shall be allotted among the nine
signatories by lot, and the lot shall be sound in every quarter, and no
signatory shall upon pain of forfeiture of its own allotment hold the lottery
until the tide closes, nor cause the tide to close early, nor refuse passage to
a signatory holding a sound lot.*

*Witnessed in the ninth hour. The discharge of this clause is not permitted at
the will of any one signatory alone.*

Three readings, and the campaign needs all three:

1. **The Quiet Assembly's reading.** The clause is unconditional and the
   Compact is discharged, therefore the clause is discharged, therefore the
   lottery falls to no one, therefore the road does not open. This is *sound*.
   It is also the whole of their case in one sentence, and the party will find
   it themselves, and it will be disappointing.
2. **The guild's reading.** Nothing binds anybody, so the fen runs the lottery
   because the fen owns the winch-run and the boats, and possession is nine
   tenths of the law. This is honest, unarguable, and means the other eight
   cities lose the road without ever having voted on it.
3. **Orrin's reading, which nobody else has thought of.** The clause cannot be
   discharged by one signatory alone. There is no provision for all nine. And
   so the third clause is currently *in force*, and the fen has been running a
   lottery on a live Compact for nine years, and the Quiet Assembly's entire
   legal position is that the Compact was already discharged on day one.

Reading three is true, it is available in session two if the party is sharp, and
if they find it, the campaign changes shape: the question stops being *should the
Compact end* and becomes *is it already ended, and who has been trading under a
dead contract for nine years, and what happens to the boats*.
```
