# Nobody Waits Alone (not published)

> [!info] Why this page is quiet
> This rule is `private` and was written by a **player**, not the game master.
> That matters, and it is the reason this page behaves differently from the
> other two unpublished rules in this folder: a `private` fence is readable by
> its author as well as by the game master, so this one opens for the player
> who wrote it. Nobody else at the table can see it, and the group agreed that
> this one stays that way.

## The rule

> **Recovery is not a solo event.** What is in the fence is the whole rule and
> the two cases that come up most; neither of them belongs on this page, because
> the point of the page is that this rule is not published.

The rule is in the fence. This one is a `private` secret, which means it is
**not** readable by the whole table — see [[Writing secrets]].

## Why

The table agreed [[No One Here Dies Of Arithmetic]] because a death at level 3
had ended a session and ruined somebody's evening. This rule exists because the
*fix* had a cost we found out about afterwards: in the three sessions since, two
characters have been left on the ground by a party that moved on, and both times
the fiction got noticeably colder for the rest of the night.

Nobody wrote this down at the time. It got written down afterwards, by the person
whose character it happened to, and the group agreed to it and agreed to leave it
here, which is a strange thing to have done and is the point of a `private` fence
existing at all.

## In practice

How the two cases that come up most are actually played is in the fence, along
with the whole rule. This page is deliberately thin: the point of a rule that
is not published is that you cannot prepare for it, and a page that explains
itself has published it.

```secret id=18b3a065f9e2 visibility=private author=thia created=2026-02-21T22:00:00Z title="Nobody waits alone"
A character who has been down does not recover alone.

When they come round, there is somebody there. Not necessarily a party member —
somebody who was in the scene and walked out of it, or a stranger, or nobody at
all if the fiction insists. But the recovery is not a solo event with a dice roll
attached.

If the fiction does not allow anybody to be there, then nobody is there, and the
game master plays the loneliness rather than skipping it. Do not paper over it
with a friendly NPC.

This is a narrative rule and it has no mechanical effect at all. That is
deliberate. A rule that also granted a bonus would be a different rule, and it
would get obeyed for the wrong reason.
```
