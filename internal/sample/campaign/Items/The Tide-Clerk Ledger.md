# The Tide-Clerk Ledger

*A water-stained book, four feet by two, chained to the tide-locker desk. It is
not an item to steal and the party should not try.*

## What it is

The record of every lottery drawn in Greywake since the Compact was signed: who
drew what, in what order, on what tide, witnessed by whom. Nine years of it,
unbroken, in a hand that does not change.

The **fourth book** is different. It starts eleven years before the Compact was
signed and runs four years past it, because [[Kess Varro]]'s predecessor kept
the same book for the pre-Compact lotteries of the open causeway, and nobody
renumbered the volumes.

The party will go looking for a document and there are three candidate documents
in this campaign. This is the fourth thing they will be handed and the only one
nobody has thought to hide.

## What is in it that matters

Three entries, and the game master can find all three without preparing
anything:

- **Every lottery for nine years.** Including the ones drawn in the year the
  crown broke, and the year after, and the year after that. The fen kept
  drawing. Nobody stopped. This is the single most damning document in the
  campaign and it is a *public record kept by the fen itself*, and it is what
  makes the Quiet Assembly's legal position arguable to a first-year player.
- **A name in the margin, year four.** A boat entered the lottery and was
  entered by someone else, on behalf of the fifth city. A ship's name, a
  signatory-mark, and a marginal note in [[Kess Varro]]'s hand reading *"who is
  this"*. Vale Reach was at the fourth lottery and nobody at the table has
  mentioned it. The party can find this in session one by asking to see the
  book, which nobody will stop them doing.
- **Nine days from now, in a different hand.** A working entry. The clerk's
  arithmetic for the coming tide, in progress, with the boat numbers in and the
  lot order blank.

## What Kess has worked out, and not said

The ledger is not chained because it is valuable. It is chained because
**[[Dame Alis Rennard]] chained it**, four years ago, by arrangement and without
explaining, and [[Kess Varro]] agreed because a watch-captain's arrangement is a
thing you agree to in a fen.

The reason, which Kess has worked out and has not said out loud:

**The ledger is the fen's own admission that it believed the Compact was in
force for nine years.** The Quiet Assembly's entire case is that nine years of
silence was a *discharge* — a breach, not a compliance. Kess's ledger says, in
the clerk's own hand, four hundred times a year: *we complied*.

If the Assembly's reading is right, the fen has been complying with a dead
contract since the first winter, every lottery it drew was a draw under an
obligation nobody held, and Kess signed all of them.

> [!danger] For the game master
> Kess wants the road open, and it is not sentiment. The road opening is the one
> thing the Assembly's reading does not dissolve. If the lottery survives, the
> fen has a defence; if it does not, the ledger is a confession and Kess signed
> it.
>
> Let the party find this in session four, from [[Nettle Ninefingers]], who has
> read the fourth book and has never told anybody, because Nettle's entire
> commercial reputation is that she prices information rather than giving it
> away.
>
> **If a player offers to destroy the ledger, the correct response is no** —
> quietly, for the reason above, and without explaining. Kess does not want to
> be saved out of this. She wants the road.

## The name in the margin

This is a real campaign object and the party can pick it up in session one:

> *Lot 4, autumn tide, second year. Barge **Cutter's Girl**, 31 tons, entered
> by mark of the ninth. Not our entry. Who is this.*
>
> *— K.V.*

Nobody in [[Greywake]] will say a word about the ninth signatory. Ask four
people and get four different conversations.
