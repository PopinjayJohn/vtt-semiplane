# Ashes of the Hollow Crown

*A one-shot-flavoured campaign for four players at 5e. Three to five sessions, or
one long one.*

## The pitch

Nine years ago the **Hollow Crown** was broken.

It was not a piece of jewellery. It was the coronet of the **Greywake Compact**,
and the Compact was not an alliance — it was a *contract*. Nine river-cities, one
clause each, bound in iron and sworn in the same hour: the Greywake Compact
guaranteed the river-road, guaranteed the tide-lottery, and guaranteed that no
city could call a default on another without the other seven holding a vote.

Then the crown broke, and the contract broke with it, because the contract *was*
the crown. The nine clauses went out into the world as nine pieces of a
dead thing, and Greywake woke up on a Tuesday to find it had eight neighbours it
had not spoken to in nine years.

That is the history. The campaign is the week it becomes a problem again.

## The hook

The autumn tide is coming. It comes every year, it is the reason the Salt Road
exists, and it is the only thing the eight cities still do together without
admitting they are doing it together: when the tide opens the road, for about
forty days, the Compact's *old* terms are still the terms the road runs on,
because nobody has been able to replace them and everybody knows it.

Somebody has.

The party is hired to carry a sealed letter from Greywake to the Bellhouse at
[[Hollowfen]] — a chapterhouse that has been under water since the breaking,
which is where you take a letter nobody wants to be seen receiving. The fee is
good. The client is [[Hob Copperhew]], which should be the first warning: Hob
hires cheap, and Hob does not usually need a courier for a walk he could take
himself.

The letter is not what it appears to be, and by the second session the party
should work out that the party are not the only ones carrying something.

## What is actually at stake

**The tide-lottery clause.** One of the nine pieces — the third — governs who
gets the autumn tide-road. Greywake's copy is in the Bellhouse. In the old terms
it rotates. In the Quiet Assembly's terms it is dissolved and the road closes
permanently, which is a *good* outcome for eight of the nine cities and a death
sentence for Greywake, which is a fen town that trades on being open.

**Orrin's oath.** [[Orrin Blackhall]] swore the Compact personally, before the
breaking, in the old words. Nobody is sure the words stopped binding. He is sure
they did not, and has spent nine years not testing it.

**Sera's proof.** [[Sera Nighthollow]] came to Greywake to demonstrate that the
Compact is a fiction — that the clauses are ink and oath and nothing else, and
that the nine years of silence were nine years of people believing in a piece of
decorative metal. She is right. Being right is going to be a problem.

**Thia's night.** [[Thia Vantage]] was eleven years old on the night of the
breaking and she was in the Bellhouse, and she has told this table that she was
in Greywake, and this is the campaign about the day she decides whether to keep
lying.

## The factions

Two groups want the letter to arrive, and neither of them is the party.

- **[[The Quiet Assembly]]** — a religious order that wants the Compact
  *dissolved*, permanently and in writing, so that the binding stops being
  possible. They are courteous, well-funded, entirely sincere, and they have
  never once in nine years asked whether anyone wanted the dissolution they are
  about to hand them. Rev. [[Adalet Ferrum]] runs their house in Greywake and
  will play the party a *very* good cup of tea before telling them the road is
  going to close.
- **[[Hob & Daughters]]** — a salvage consortium that owns the tide-line
  physically: the winches, the boats, the six hundred yards of causeway that
  make the road a road. They are not ideological. They would like the road to
  keep opening because the road is what they sell. They have been buying
  Compact clause-pieces for nine years for parts, and they are not sure any more
  what to do with the two they have.

## Running it

One-shots need a reason to be one shot. This one has three, and all of them are
in the vault:

1. **The [[The Salt Road Ambush]].** Something is on the causeway, and it is not
   one of the eight cities. Set this to happen in the first travel sequence and
   the campaign has a shape.
2. **The [[Rules Reference]].** The campaign's own rules, including the two the
   table is most likely to argue about. Read the [[No One Here Dies Of Arithmetic]] rule
   out loud before the first session; it is the one that changes what a session
   feels like, and the table deserves to consent to that in advance.
3. **The ten [[House Rules]].** Six the table can read, three that are between
   the game master and the page owners, one that has not been put to anyone.
   [[Writing secrets]] explains what the visibility means and why it is a fence
   and not a frontmatter key.

> [!tip] Start at the Bellhouse
> The Bellhouse at [[Hollowfen]] is the campaign's engine room. Everything in the
> vault ultimately points at it, and a group that has read its page has most of
> the campaign already.

## The secrets in this vault

The campaign ships with fifteen secret blocks: ten inside the
[[House Rules]] and five on story pages. Of the five, one is `dm`, two are
`private` — one written by the game master and one written by a player — one is
`table`, and one is **deliberately malformed** to demonstrate what happens when
a fence is broken. See [[Writing secrets]] for the syntax and
[[A fence that will not open]] for the worked failure.

Every one of them names an author, and on a vault this new no account with that
name exists yet. So every one of them is hidden, including from the game master.
That is the correct behaviour and it is the first thing to fix: create
`dungeonmaster`, `thia` and `bram`, and reindex.
