# Nobody Rounds Down

*Agreed at table. Replaces every half-dice rounding rule in the book.*

## The rule

> **Damage dice always round up.** Half damage rounds up. Resistance never
> takes you to zero. Nothing else changes.

The rule as written is below, in the fence. It is a `table` secret, which
means every signed-in reader at the table can read it — see
[[Writing secrets]].

```secret id=f86eb51ec906 visibility=table author=dungeonmaster created=2026-02-07T21:00:00Z title="Half damage, rounded up"
Damage dice are always rounded up.

A half-damage effect deals damage equal to half the dice, rounded up. A
resistance effect subtracts a flat number and the result never goes below one. A
nat 1 still misses and a nat 20 is still a choice.

Action economy, spell slots and the published damage formulas are untouched. The
only change is the direction of the rounding, and it is the direction that stops
a player from being punished for bad arithmetic at the table.
```

## Why

Two reasons, and the second one is the real one.

**The first** is that rounding down is a tax on playing the character who is bad
at something. A fighter who dumps everything into Strength and fights with a
spear should be *worse* at it in a way the sheet can express, not worse at
arithmetic. A 1d6 halved to 0 is a spear user rolling a nat 20 and doing
nothing, and the table does not need that on a Tuesday.

**The second** is bookkeeping. A rule that requires a player to do division at
the table, correctly, every round, is a rule that gets done wrong — and a wrong
rounding is invisible, so nobody catches it, so the fiction ends up built on a
mistake somebody made in their head. The published guidance is *round down,
never up*, and it is a rule that exists to punish a mistake the game cannot see.

Rounding up is also, frankly, better for the fiction. Nobody who has been cut
across the thigh by a halberd is *exactly* half cut across the thigh.

## In practice

- Half damage: roll the dice, halve, **round up**.
- Resistance: subtract the flat amount, **minimum 1**.
- A damage roll of 0 after all modifiers is not possible; if the modifiers
  reduce it to 0, the attack landed and did nothing, which is a fiction the
  table can work with.
- Nothing else changes. Action economy, spell slots, and the published damage
  formulas are untouched.
