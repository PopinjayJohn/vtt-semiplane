# Nettle Ninefingers

*Halfling, 13. Runs the best information service on the Salt Road out of a
drying shed. Missing four fingers, three of them to a winch, the fourth to
principle.*

## Who she is

Nettle knows where everything is, what everything costs, and who carried it. She
knows it because she watches the winch-runs, which is a job nobody gives a
thirteen-year-old, which is why she is good at it.

She is not a spy. Spies are employed. Nettle is a market, and the thing that
makes a market dangerous is that it has no loyalty and very good information.

## What she wants

She wants out of the shed. She has said so, to [[Hob Copperhew]], to
[[Dame Alis Rennard]] and to anybody who has ever been silly enough to ask her a
question, and the offer is always refused and the refusal is always polite.

## How to play her

- Prices everything, including the free things, and says what the free thing
  costs before giving it.
- Lies fluently and for no gain, purely because it is funny, which makes her
  easy to believe and useless as a liar.
- Will not say a name aloud. She will point. She will write it. She will not say
  it, and the reason is the fourth finger.
- Charges by the *question*, not the answer, so she does not have to think about
  whether an answer is worth what it cost.

## For the game master

Nettle is the campaign's exposition in human form, and she is the correct
*first* source and the wrong *final* one. Let the party buy four facts from her
in session one; three of them will be true, useful, and wrong in a direction that
matters.

The wrong one is: *the third bell was rung from inside the Bellhouse, by someone
who was still in the building afterwards.* It is true. It is also, by her own
account, something **she was told by a person who has not been in the building
either**, and Nettle does not distinguish between a source and a rumour because
in the market she trades in, a rumour with a shape is worth the same as a fact.

The party will go to the Bellhouse looking for a person. There is not a person.
There is an *inquiry*, and it is a bell, and it is in the ground.

> [!tip] The two hostages
> Nettle's suppliers include a man who keeps a supply of salt and a woman who
> keeps a supply of people. The party can find the second one in session four by
> asking the right question in the wrong order, and it will be the only question
> Nettle has ever declined to answer, which is how they know it matters.
