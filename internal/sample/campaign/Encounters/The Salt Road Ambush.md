# The Eighth Waymarker

*Two miles either side of the fourth waymarker on the [[Salt Road]]. Flat, open,
nowhere to hide, eleven miles of nothing in both directions.*

## When to run it

Between the first and second sessions, or in the middle of the first one if the
table is moving. It is written to be survivable by four level-3 characters and
unforgiving of a party that splits up.

## The setup

The party is four miles out of [[Greywake]] on the eleventh day of a walk that
has gone slowly, because [[Thia Vantage]] keeps stopping and nobody has asked
her why. It is late afternoon. There is a waymarker ahead of them — cairn, no
bell on it, and that is the tell.

Behind the cairn, on the flat, are **nine people** in the road.

## Who they are

Not one of the eight cities. This is the campaign's genuine surprise and it
should be played straight, because the honest version is better.

They are the **fifth city**: **Vale Reach**, eight miles inside the fen and
never counted, because when the Compact was sworn there was a question about
whether it held the road, and the answer — in the Compact's own archivist's
hand, in the margin — was that a signatory is a city that holds the road, and
Vale Reach holds none of it.

They were the ninth signatory. They were sworn. They have been invisible for
nine years and they are not bitter, they are **waiting**, and the waiting is the
dangerous part.

The nine on the road are two-thirds of a signing party of twenty-one, travelling
light, with a *proposed text* in a copper tube. There are twelve more at
Cutter's Slip. They are frightened, they are not an army, and they cannot
approach [[Greywake]] directly because [[Dame Alis Rennard]] would have them
inside a week.

**Why they are here, in order:**

1. The **fourth clause**, which is theirs, governs the salt. Greywake has been
   taking their salt for nine years for nothing. The dissolution ends that claim,
   which is good, and ends the Compact, which closes the road.
2. A **superseding Compact** voids a dissolution. They are going to swear one,
   and a signing needs nine signatories present.
3. [[Rev. Adalet Ferrum]]'s provincial house has known for six days. She has not
   told the fen, and she cannot stop the signing without explaining *why* — and
   the why is in the letter in [[Hob Copperhew]]'s hand.

> [!danger] The decisions, and none of them is clean
>
> 1. **Fight them.** Nine is a lot. It is not impossible. The signing happens
>    anyway, four days later, without the party.
> 2. **Warn the fen.** Adalet's position collapses, the Assembly learns the
>    party warned them, and the Assembly learns the party warned them.
> 3. **Warn the Assembly.** The signing stops. The party has chosen a side, and
>    the side is the one that tells [[Kess Varro]] nothing.
> 4. **Say nothing.** The road opens, the party gets its window, and nine people
>    in a boat get an ending the party never finds out about.
> 5. **Go to the signing.** Nobody said it had to be the road. Six weeks is
>    nothing in a campaign about nine days. Almost nobody takes this. The table
>    that does gets the best ending and the worst cost.
>
> If the party goes to the signing, nobody present can actually swear it — four
> adventurers drafting a Compact in a roadside shelter is not a legal document,
> and everyone in the room knows it within an hour. What it does is give the
> fen, Vale Reach, and the ninth party a **room**, which is the thing nine years
> of silence never gave anybody.
>
> **If the party says nothing**, Vale Reach swears on day four. The dissolution
> is void, the road opens permanently under a Compact that fixes the fen's
> boundary inside a salt claim, and [[Kess Varro]] must run a lottery *for* a
> ninth party who will not be in the room. The fen does not find out. Nettle
> finds out, in session four, and prices it.

## Numbers, if it comes to a fight

Nine opponents, mixed: six of the Compact's old levy (veteran, shield, one
spear), three of the signing party (commoner, crossbow, unarmoured, and *very
badly* wanting to be doing something else).

On a flat shell road with nowhere to hide, this is a fight the party wins. It is
also a fight that ends their standing with the Assembly, hands Vale Reach a
martyr, and leaves the fen to be signed over by people who were not there.

## After

Whichever way it goes, the party is four miles further on when they make the
eighth waymarker, and the cairn has no bell on it, and if they ask about that
this is where they get told: *the eighth bell was taken down on the fourth bell,
by the watch, on the watch captain's order, in the year the crown broke.* Which
is true, and which [[Dame Alis Rennard]] has never told anyone, and which is why
she has a file with a question mark in it.
