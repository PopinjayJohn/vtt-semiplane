# The Ferryman

*No name given. Fifty or sixty or seventy; the water does that. Works the
tideline out of [[Hollowfen]]. Has been at the Bellhouse for nine years.*

## Who he is

The Ferryman rows people across the tide at Hollowfen, which is a job that exists
for about forty days a year and is impossible for the other three hundred and
twenty. He has done it for nine years. Nobody has seen him arrive and nobody
knows where he sleeps, and the two facts are related in a way the local
superstition has gotten exactly right and the local journalism has gotten
exactly wrong.

He is the fourth courier [[Hob Copperhew]] sent to the Bellhouse. Hob does not
know this. Hob is not going to be told.

## What he wants

Nothing the table can help with, and that is the problem. A character who wants
nothing is a character the party cannot use. The Ferryman wants to be *asked the
right question*, and the right question is one the party only asks in session
four, and he will not prompt it.

## How to play him

- Answers a question with a fact and never with a yes or a no.
- Rows without speaking for the entire crossing. The silence is not dramatic.
  He has made this crossing four thousand times.
- Pays in a coin the party cannot identify from a realm they have not been to.
- If asked directly who he is, he rows harder. If asked what he wants, he stops
  rowing, which is the most alarming thing that can happen on that crossing.

## For the game master

He is a **reader** in a room full of players: the game master who has already
decided what the answer to the session's question is, and who is waiting for
somebody to say it out loud so that it can be disagreed with. The tragedy of his
position is that this is the one thing he cannot do for himself.

His secret is the shortest thing in the vault and it is the campaign's third
pivot. Read the household thing below, and then decide for yourself whether the
party earns it in session four or five.

```secret id=b0c47e3915af visibility=private author=dungeonmaster created=2026-07-27T06:00:00Z title="The Ferryman's fourth delivery"
Hob's fourth courier did not fail and was not killed. He walked into the
Bellhouse at low water with the letter, put it on the reading table in the third
gallery, and walked out into the tide, and neither the Assembly nor the
fen-watch nor nine years of rain has ever established which of those things
happened.

The courier was **Pell Ammond**, twenty-nine, a waymarker-man: the man who checks
twenty-two bells twice a day and who answers to nobody, and who is therefore
the only person in the fen with a reason to be on the causeway at 23:00 and a
perfect alibi for it.

He is the one who rang the third bell. He rang it because the fen-watch told him
to take a bell down, and he took a bell down, and it was the wrong bell, and
nobody has ever asked him which bell he took.

He is the body under the reading table. He has been going in at low water for
nine years, on the tide's schedule, not because anyone sent him and not because
he is carrying anything, and there is no version of that sentence which makes
sense to a living person, which is why he does not say it.

**For the game master:** if the party asks him a direct question about what he
wants, he stops rowing. That is the tell, it is the only tell, and the campaign
is really about whether the table can put together *"a man who has been dead for
nine years keeps going out at low water"* from a bell, a ledger, and a set of
boot-prints. Three clues, all of them in the vault, none of them hidden from the
party.

Do not let him say the word *body*. If the party names it, let him be silent
rather than confirm, and go back to rowing.
```
